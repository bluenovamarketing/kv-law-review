Practice overview

Evidence before conclusions

Equitable relief requires
specific facts and precision.

New Jersey’s Chancery Division, General Equity, hears matters in which the principal right or relief sought is equitable in nature. These cases may involve urgent protection, business control, fiduciary obligations, restrictive covenants, accountings or other remedies that a damages award alone may not address.

The party seeking relief must connect the requested order to a developed record. Kennedy Vuernick organizes the chronology, governing agreements, communications, financial evidence and practical consequences so the court can evaluate what relief is legally and factually supported.

Chancery matters often move quickly and require careful compliance after an order is entered. Strategy must account for immediate risk, discovery, ongoing business operations and the form of final relief.

This page provides general information and is not legal advice. It does not evaluate any specific person, transaction, investigation or dispute.

Legal and practical context

Appointments depend on
the court’s specific mandate.

General Equity judges manage cases involving primarily non-monetary relief and may use case-specific orders to protect interests, obtain reliable information or administer a process. The title, scope and authority of any independent appointee depend on the governing law and order.

An appointee’s neutrality is operational, not rhetorical. Communications, information handling, decisions, expenses and reports should all remain within the mandate and be documented for the court and parties as required.

A clear appointment framework should identify the source of authority, scope of work, reporting recipients, information-access rules, compensation procedures and deadlines. Those boundaries reduce ambiguity and support accountable administration.

General information

Chancery Appointments & Litigation
questions.

These answers provide a general orientation. They do not assess a particular matter or create an attorney-client relationship.

What is the Chancery Division, General Equity?

It is the part of the New Jersey Superior Court that hears matters in which equitable rights or relief are primary, even when legal relief is also requested.

What kinds of relief may be considered equitable?

Depending on the case, equitable relief may include injunctions, accountings, specific performance or court-supervised remedies. The standards and availability are fact-specific.

What does a court-appointed professional do?

The court’s order controls. A role may involve defined observation, investigation, administration, preservation or reporting tasks, but no authority should be assumed beyond the mandate.

How do Chancery litigation and monitorships relate?

Both may involve structured oversight and reporting, but a monitorship and a Chancery appointment are not interchangeable. The source, purpose and scope of authority must be explained precisely.
