The legal framework

Government funds and programs

A framework for addressing
certain false claims.

The federal False Claims Act can impose liability when a person knowingly submits—or causes another to submit—a false claim to the government. The statute also addresses certain false records or statements connected to a false claim.

Other provisions concern the improper avoidance of obligations owed to the government, sometimes described as reverse false claims, as well as conspiracy. Federal and state laws differ, and their application depends on the specific facts and governing law.

Kennedy Vuernick evaluates potential False Claims Act matters through close review of the available information, the relevant government payment or funding relationship and the procedures that may apply.

This overview is general information, not legal advice. False Claims Act requirements and procedures can vary by jurisdiction and circumstance.

Knowingly presenting or causing a false or fraudulent claim for government payment or approval.

02

False records

Certain false records or statements that are material to a false or fraudulent claim.

03

Reverse false claims

Certain conduct involving the improper avoidance or reduction of an obligation owed to the government.

04

Conspiracy

Agreements to engage in conduct that may violate provisions of the False Claims Act.

Qui tam actions

A specialized process with
distinct roles.

The federal False Claims Act permits a private party—commonly called a relator—to bring a qui tam action in the name of the government. These matters follow specialized procedures.

General information

Questions about the
False Claims Act.

These answers provide a high-level orientation only. They do not evaluate any specific matter or replace legal advice.

What is a qui tam action?

A qui tam action is a lawsuit brought by a private party in the name of the government under the False Claims Act. The person bringing the action is commonly called a relator.

Is a federal qui tam complaint filed under seal?

Federal law provides for a qui tam complaint to be filed under seal initially, allowing the government time to review the allegations and decide how it will proceed.

Does the government participate in every matter?

No. The government decides whether to intervene after reviewing the matter. The procedural options after that decision depend on the governing law and circumstances.

Why can early legal guidance matter?

False Claims Act matters involve specialized legal and procedural requirements. A careful review can help determine what information may be relevant and what steps should—or should not—be taken before a filing decision.
