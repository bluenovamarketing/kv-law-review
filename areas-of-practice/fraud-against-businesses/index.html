Practice overview

Evidence before conclusions

Trace the conduct.
Connect the evidence.

A business may first discover fraud through a payment discrepancy, an unusual vendor relationship, a missing asset or an employee report. The first signal rarely explains the entire problem. Counsel must determine which records matter, who controlled the relevant decisions and whether the conduct reflects an isolated event or a broader course of dealing.

Kennedy Vuernick works with clients to frame the investigation around practical questions: what occurred, who participated, how the organization was affected and what evidence is available now. That focused beginning helps preserve information before it is lost and prevents the matter from becoming a collection of disconnected documents.

When litigation is appropriate, the firm develops claims and defenses from the evidence rather than assumptions. Potential remedies, interim protection, settlement, trial preparation and collection considerations are evaluated in light of the governing law and the client’s business objectives.

Scope / evidence / analysis / strategy

Evidence-driven development

Build an evidence map.
Then test the claims.

Documents and data. Organize contracts, invoices, bank records, accounting entries and system data by issue and time period.

Communications. Evaluate emails, messages, approvals and instructions for what decision-makers knew and represented.

People and relationships. Identify roles, access, financial connections and witnesses with first-hand knowledge.

Loss and remedy analysis. Examine causation, measurable harm, asset location and the practical value of available relief.

A deliberate process

A disciplined path
from discovery to decision.

The sequence changes with the facts, but each phase should answer a defined legal or business question.

  1. 01

    Stabilize the situation

    Identify immediate evidence, access, operational and limitation-period concerns without assuming the allegation is proved.

  2. 02

    Investigate proportionally

    Collect and analyze the records and interviews most likely to confirm, narrow or disprove the suspected conduct.

  3. 03

    Evaluate legal pathways

    Assess viable claims, defenses, parties, forums, interim relief and litigation risks under the developed record.

  4. 04

    Pursue the chosen strategy

    Prepare for negotiation, motion practice, trial or other resolution while continuing to test the evidence.

Legal and practical context

Commercial disputes become fraud cases
only when the facts support it.

Not every breach of contract, loss or accounting error is fraud. A credible fraud claim generally requires proof of specific conduct and legal elements that differ from an ordinary commercial disagreement.

That distinction affects pleadings, discovery, available remedies and settlement posture. Careful early analysis helps a business avoid overstating a claim while preserving the ability to act decisively when the evidence warrants it.

Kennedy Vuernick’s approach is to separate suspicion from proof, organize the record and develop the legal theory that best fits the verified facts.

General information

Fraud Against Businesses
questions.

These answers provide a general orientation. They do not assess a particular matter or create an attorney-client relationship.

What should a business do after discovering suspected fraud?

Preserve relevant records, restrict inappropriate access when lawful, document what is known and seek advice before confronting participants or making public accusations. The right sequence depends on the circumstances.

What records may matter in a business fraud investigation?

Contracts, invoices, bank and accounting records, system logs, approvals, communications, ownership information and witness accounts may all be relevant. Counsel can help define a proportionate collection.

Does every fraud investigation lead to a lawsuit?

No. An investigation may confirm a claim, narrow the issue, identify a contractual remedy or show that litigation is not the best path. Strategy should follow the evidence.

Can a business seek urgent court relief?

Some matters may justify temporary or equitable relief, but the requirements are fact-specific. Counsel must evaluate the evidence, risk and applicable procedural rules.
