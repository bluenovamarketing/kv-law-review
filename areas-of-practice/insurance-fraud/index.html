Practice overview

From isolated claim to complete record

Complex schemes require
connected analysis.

Suspected insurance fraud is rarely established by one document or one unusual claim. The relevant picture may emerge only after claims, treatment records, billing patterns, ownership information, financial relationships and witness accounts are examined together.

Kennedy Vuernick works with insurer legal teams, claims professionals and Special Investigation Units to define the questions, organize the record and determine what the evidence supports. The firm’s current practice places particular emphasis on significant matters involving healthcare providers.

Each investigation is shaped around the suspected conduct and the client’s objectives. Some matters call for targeted legal analysis or pre-suit development; others require a broader investigation, expert input, pleadings, discovery and litigation.

This page provides general information about the firm’s insurance fraud practice. It is not legal advice and does not describe the merits of any specific matter.

Evidence-driven case development

Build the record.
Test the theory.

Claims data. Analyze billing, payment, referral and provider data for patterns that may not be visible claim by claim.

Medical records. Review documentation for consistency among reported conditions, treatment, timing and services billed.

Financial and business relationships. Examine ownership, management, referral and payment connections that may explain how participants interact.

Expert analysis. Work with appropriate professionals when medical, technical, accounting or data issues require specialized interpretation.

Investigation through litigation

A disciplined path
from question to action.

The appropriate sequence depends on the matter. The firm’s method is designed to preserve flexibility while keeping the legal theory tied to the developing record.

  1. 01

    Define the scope

    Clarify the suspected conduct, relevant participants, available information and the client’s operational and legal objectives.

  2. 02

    Develop the evidence

    Collect and organize claims data, records, communications, testimony and public or financial information relevant to the questions presented.

  3. 03

    Evaluate the law

    Assess what the evidence may support under the applicable causes of action, procedural rules and available remedies.

  4. 04

    Determine whether the matter calls for further investigation, pre-suit action, litigation, negotiated resolution or another practical path.

The New Jersey context

Insurance fraud involves
civil and enforcement questions.

New Jersey’s Insurance Fraud Prevention Act is part of a broader legal framework addressing suspected insurance fraud. Depending on the circumstances, an insurer’s matter may also implicate other statutes, regulations, common-law claims, contractual issues or administrative requirements.

The New Jersey Office of the Insurance Fraud Prosecutor coordinates statewide anti-insurance-fraud efforts and accepts industry referrals. A private carrier’s civil investigation and litigation strategy, however, involves its own evidentiary, legal and business considerations.

Kennedy Vuernick evaluates those considerations in context. The firm does not assume that an anomaly proves fraud; it asks what the larger record shows and which legal path, if any, fits the evidence.

General information

Insurance fraud litigation
questions.

These answers provide a general orientation. They do not evaluate a specific claim, investigation or legal strategy.

What does insurance fraud litigation involve?

It may involve investigating suspected misrepresentations or schemes, developing documentary and testimonial evidence, and pursuing or defending legal claims under the applicable facts and law.

How can outside counsel support an insurance Special Investigation Unit?

Outside counsel can help define legal issues, preserve and evaluate evidence, coordinate appropriate experts, assess potential remedies and develop a litigation strategy that reflects the carrier’s objectives.

Why can claims data and medical records matter in healthcare fraud investigations?

Individual claims may reveal little in isolation. Reviewing claims data and medical records together can help identify patterns, inconsistencies, relationships and questions that warrant closer investigation.

When should a carrier consider involving insurance fraud counsel?

A carrier may wish to involve counsel when suspected conduct raises legal, evidentiary or strategic questions that extend beyond ordinary claims handling. The appropriate timing depends on the matter.
