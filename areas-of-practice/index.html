Practice overview

One method, distinct legal pathways

Understand the problem.
Choose the right path.

Fraud allegations and high-stakes disputes rarely arrive as complete legal claims. They begin with a troubling payment, a government inquiry, an internal report, an unusual business relationship or a need for immediate court intervention. The first task is to identify the legal and factual questions that matter.

Kennedy Vuernick approaches each matter through its evidence: records, communications, transactions, decision-making authority and witness knowledge. That disciplined process helps distinguish suspicion from proof and keeps the strategy aligned with the client’s goals.

The practice pages below address different clients, forums and forms of relief. Some matters may touch more than one area. The correct route depends on the verified facts, applicable law and procedural posture—not a label chosen at the outset.

This page provides general information, not legal advice. Practice descriptions do not predict the evaluation or outcome of any matter.

Scope / preserve / analyze / act

A shared working method

The strategy follows
the verified facts.

Define the question. Separate immediate risks from issues that require deeper factual or legal development.

Preserve the record. Identify documents, data, communications and witnesses before information becomes unavailable.

Test competing explanations. Evaluate what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain and which legal pathway fits.

Act with purpose. Align investigation, negotiation, litigation or independent work with a defined objective.

Finding the right page

Start with the issue.
Not the label.

Related practices can overlap without becoming interchangeable.

  1. 01

    Who needs counsel?

    A business, an individual, a potential whistleblower, an organization or a court-directed participant may require a different analysis.

  2. 02

    Whose funds or duties are involved?

    Government programs, insurance claims, private transactions and fiduciary or governance concerns create distinct legal frameworks.

  3. 03

    What stage has the matter reached?

    An initial concern, active investigation, filed litigation or oversight mandate changes what must happen next.

  4. 04

    What relief or work is needed?

    Fact development, damages, equitable relief, defense, reporting and ongoing monitoring call for different tools.
