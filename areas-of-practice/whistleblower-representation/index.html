Practice overview

Evidence before conclusions

Information becomes actionable
only through careful analysis.

A person may encounter troubling billing, contracting, grant, healthcare or procurement information through work or another professional relationship. The documents may appear significant, but context, source, reliability and legal materiality all matter.

Kennedy Vuernick helps potential whistleblowers organize what they know, distinguish first-hand evidence from inference and understand possible reporting pathways. That review should occur before records are taken, communications are made or procedural deadlines are assumed.

Some information may relate to the federal or a state False Claims Act; other concerns may belong with an inspector general, regulator, law-enforcement agency, employer process or different legal framework. Counsel can explain options while protecting confidentiality to the extent the law permits.

This page provides general information and is not legal advice. It does not evaluate any specific person, transaction, investigation or dispute.

Scope / evidence / analysis / strategy

Evidence-driven development

Preserve context.
Evaluate the framework.

Listen and organize. Develop a chronology of the conduct, the person’s role and the source of each material fact.

Review available evidence. Assess documents and data lawfully available to the client without directing improper access or removal.

Map the legal options. Consider False Claims Act procedures, agency channels and other frameworks that may apply.

Plan communications. Determine what should be shared, with whom, in what form and at what stage based on the chosen path.

A deliberate process

A careful path
from concern to informed choice.

The process is confidential and fact-specific. No one should assume that information automatically supports a claim, reward or protected status.

  1. 01

    Initial consultation

    Discuss the information at a high level and complete conflict and engagement steps before confidential details are exchanged.

  2. 02

    Evidence assessment

    Identify the strongest facts, gaps, sources, affected programs and questions requiring further legal research.

  3. 03

    Procedure and risk review

    Explain relevant filing, seal, disclosure, cooperation, timing and retaliation considerations.

  4. 04

    Client decision

    Help the client choose whether and how to proceed, then support the selected lawful process.

Legal and practical context

Whistleblower representation and FCA litigation
are connected—not identical.

The False Claims Act permits certain private persons to bring qui tam actions in the government’s name. Federal procedures include important requirements, including filing a qui tam complaint under seal. State laws and other reporting programs may differ.

Whistleblower counsel begins earlier and more broadly: evaluating information, source, confidentiality, reporting options and personal risk. A False Claims Act case is one possible legal pathway, not the definition of every whistleblower concern.

This page intentionally does not discuss the firm’s actual whistleblower matters, clients, parties, recoveries or outcomes. Any representation depends on the facts, law, conflicts and a written engagement.

General information

Whistleblower Representation
questions.

These answers provide a general orientation. They do not assess a particular matter or create an attorney-client relationship.

What information is helpful for an initial whistleblower consultation?

A concise chronology, the government program or funds involved, the client’s role and a description of lawfully available records can help counsel understand the issue. Do not send confidential documents before the firm confirms how to proceed.

Is every report of misconduct a False Claims Act matter?

No. The FCA concerns particular conduct involving government claims, money or property. Other concerns may involve different statutes, agencies or internal processes.

Why does the seal requirement matter?

Federal qui tam complaints are filed under seal under the statutory procedure, allowing the government an opportunity to investigate. Procedural advice should be obtained before filing or public disclosure.

Can counsel promise a recovery or government intervention?

No. Outcomes, government decisions and any potential relator share depend on law and facts beyond counsel’s control. This page makes no outcome or recovery representation.
